One main problem I have encountered in my balancing_sim tests is that an overwhelming opponent (High Build) can never be undefeated by an underpowered creature (Low Build) due to the nearly deterministic nature of the systems in place. One moment that is satisfying in a game is playing through a David and Goliath story: being clearly underpowered and through wit, technique and luck overcoming unfavorable odds.
One must question whether this is diagetically relevant: I want the player to feel like they are creating a monstrosity through the optimization-focused lens of a game, to reward them when they haven't developed that monstrosity enough (since build has a weak but present connection to how much of a monstrocity any creature is: extra parts that come through fusion add a lot to the build) with an everpresent chance to win or to punish them when they have indeed created an optimized overpowered mutant with a chance to lose: that might defeat the initial purpose. 
The possibility of adding luck as a feature exists anyway, and in the search for a "how?", always with an hollistic method in mind, one might answer the difficulties that the "why?" poses.

To bridge the gap between high and low build, we might give a lower build a higher chance, on a randomly chosen event, at achieving it's provisory end.

Say we add a chance to crit: A lower build unit might have a higher chance to crit than a high build unit. The actual weight of the influence of build will of course be something to test. It is a simple and time-tested solution. And simplicity might be key.

But I also believe any full feature must be something that can be used by any playing philosophy and to indulge this, a more robust and complex system might be a possibility.
